# Report — 2026-09-07

## Rentosertib shows biological age reversal across six proteomic aging clocks

Insilico Medicine published in Nature Biotechnology (Sep 7 2026) the first clinical evaluation of an AI-designed drug for an AI-discovered target using aging biomarkers. Analyzing 12-week longitudinal Olink proteomic data from 42 IPF patients in the Phase IIa trial, six independently developed proteomic aging clocks (ProtAge, OrganAge, PAC, ipfP3GPT, PAOPAC, and one more) from Harvard, Oxford, PKU, and Insilico unanimously indicated rentosertib reversed predicted biological age. Peak effect at Week 4 in the 30 mg BID group: approximately 3-4 years reversal, up to 6 years in one clock. FVC improvement showed dose-dependent reversal aligned with proteomic age reversal. The anti-aging effects operated partially independent of respiratory benefits — the dose for greatest lung function improvement differed from the strongest age-reversal signal. Mechanistically, rentosertib acted as a senomorphic agent, suppressing EREG, ESM1, IGFBP4, ITGA2, MMP10, MMP13, SPP1 while downregulating RTK-PI3K and RAS-ERK pathways. Michael Levitt (Nobel laureate) commented: "What convinces me is not the size of the effect but the agreement." Data deposited at CNCB (OMIX008341), code open-source on GitHub. First scalable blueprint for dual-purpose clinical trials integrating geroscience endpoints. Presentation at Nature conference in Paris Sep 8. https://insilico.com/news/rnt0709261-rentosertib-proteomic-aging-clocks

## Novartis and BMS pause autoimmune CAR-T trials after patient deaths

Novartis suspended eight autoimmune and neurology trials of rapcabtagene autoleucel (rap-cel) after three patients died from immune effector cell-associated hemophagocytic syndrome (IEC-HS). Cancer studies of rap-cel continue unaffected. Bristol Myers Squibb separately paused enrollment in autoimmune trials of zolacabtagene autoleucel (zola-cel) after transient and reversible inflammatory events — no deaths reported. Both are CD19-directed autologous CAR-T therapies built on rapid manufacturing platforms. Analysts (William Blair) hypothesized rapid manufacturing may produce cells that expand more aggressively in patients. Same day (Sep 1), Roche paid $75M upfront for Simcere Zaiming's SIM0660, a preclinical trispecific antibody (CD79a/CD19/CD3) targeting the same B cell biology — up to $1.53B in milestones. ArsenalBio also cut workforce to pivot entirely to in vivo CAR-T. Signals autoimmune CAR-T safety profile is more complex than oncology setting, and industry is losing patience with ex vivo cell therapy outside oncology. https://www.biospace.com/drug-development/novartis-pauses-car-t-studies-due-to-3-deaths-bms-follows-suit-after-safety-events

## AF3-ReD: Breaking AlphaFold3's single-conformation limitation

Researchers at Institute for Molecular Science (IMS) and SOKENDAI developed AF3-ReD, a method that introduces a repulsive force between predicted structures in AlphaFold3's diffusion generative model, enabling sampling of multiple conformational states that AF3's default settings rarely capture. Published in JACS Au (2026). Demonstrated on F1β subunit of ATP synthase: AF3 predicts only the open conformation even for ATP-bound form, while AF3-ReD samples open, closed, and intermediate conformations. Potential beyond structure prediction — diffusion generative models are used in protein and drug design, so the repulsive bias approach could enable more diverse design. Addresses the widely recognized limitation that AF3 predicts only a single conformation for many proteins, limiting applicability to drug design where conformational switching is functionally critical. https://dx.doi.org/10.1021/jacsau.6c00596

## AI-designed enzymes for PFAS degradation

Scientists are using deep-learning protein folding algorithms to design novel enzymes capable of breaking down PFAS ("forever chemicals"). No naturally occurring enzyme evolved to degrade synthetic PFAS, so AI reverse-engineers protein structures that can bind and catalytically shatter carbon-fluorine bonds. The EPA views this as the only viable solution for deeply contaminated municipal reservoirs, as traditional carbon filtration merely traps rather than destroys the chemicals. Signals AI protein design expanding beyond therapeutics into environmental remediation. https://metapress.com/ai-protein-folding-algorithms-design-enzymes-to-destroy-chemicals-2026/

## Convergen raises $15M Seed+ for TrimTAC protein degradation platform

Suzhou-based Convergen closed $15M Seed+ financing (MPCi-led, with LAV, BioTrack Capital) to advance TrimTAC targeted protein degradation platform for neurodegenerative and autoimmune diseases. Total raised: $25M since founding. TrimTAC leverages TRIM21's unique properties to selectively degrade pathological multimeric protein aggregates — the root cause of many neurodegenerative diseases — overcoming a key limitation of traditional bifunctional degraders. ## Alumis LUMUS lupus trial misses primary endpoint

Alumis reported that the Phase 2b LUMUS trial of envudeucitinib missed its primary BICLA endpoint at week 48 in the overall population of 408 lupus patients. However, interferon signature high patients responded robustly. Plans Phase 3 focused on that subgroup with regulators.

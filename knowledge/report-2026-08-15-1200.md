### Aureka open-sources antibody AI, closes $100M Series B

**Source:** https://www.techtimes.com/articles/323933/20260811/aureka-open-sourced-antibody-ai-that-beats-alphafold-3-closes-100m-drug-discovery-round.htm

Aureka Biotechnologies (Laguna Hills, CA / Shanghai) released OpenDDE, an open-source antibody-antigen structure prediction model under Apache 2.0 license. Independent validation by Tamarind Bio found OpenDDE v1 achieving 76.1% DockQ success on FoldBench v1, compared with 47.9% for AlphaFold 3. On the 2026ARK-AB benchmark, OpenDDE posted 66.4% vs. Chai-1 (16.7%), Boltz-1 (25.0%), OpenFold3 (33.1%).

Caveat: on protein-ligand co-folding, OpenDDE scores ~0.601 vs. AF3's 0.649. The advantage is specifically in the antibody-antigen domain.

The $100M Series B brings total raised to ~$200M. Investors include Granite Asia, HighLight Capital, Qiming Venture Partners, Matrix Partners China. Aureka claims tens of millions in commercial revenue from pharma partnerships. The moat is the Lab-in-the-Loop data flywheel, not the model itself.

### NISE: Zero-shot design of drug-binding proteins (Nature)

**Source:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10670-w

A paper published in Nature (June 24, 2026) describes NISE (neural iterative selection-expansion), a method for designing small-molecule binding proteins from scratch. Two neural networks — LASErMPNN (sequence design) and a co-structure predictor (Boltz-2) — iterate in a closed loop optimizing sequence-structure-ligand self-consistency.

Results:
- **Exatecan binders**: 4/4 designs bound (100% success). Tightest binder EPIC (Kd = 120 nM), improved to 1.2 nM with two mutations. Protected drug's lactone ring from hydrolysis for days.
- **Apixaban binders**: 83% success rate (vs. 0.08% for LigandMPNN+Rosetta on identical backbones). Tightest binder APEX (Kd = 80 pM) — 10,000x tighter than prior methods, rivaling native target factor Xa.

This is a major advance in de novo protein design for drug delivery, sensing, and catalysis.

### Experiment-guided AlphaFold3 (Nature Biotechnology)

**Source:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03166-5

A Nature Biotechnology paper (June 29, 2026) introduces a framework for using AlphaFold3 to generate protein structural ensembles consistent with NMR, X-ray, and cryo-EM data. Instead of producing a single static structure, the method modifies AF3's reverse diffusion process to include gradient-based guidance from experimental likelihoods. This enables ensemble-averaged observables that agree with experimental measurements. Applicable across crystallography, NMR, and cryo-EM workflows.

### ROCKET: AF2 refinement with cryo-EM and crystallography data

**Source:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-026-03047-4

ROCKET (Nature Methods, April 2026) augments AlphaFold2/OpenFold with differentiable crystallographic and cryo-EM likelihood targets. It refines structures at inference time without retraining, steering predictions toward experimentally supported conformations. Particularly valuable for low-resolution cryo-EM maps (<4-5 Å). Works by optimizing embedded MSA cluster profiles in coevolutionary embedding space.

## Notes for buffers

The field's center of gravity this month is the convergence of AI agents with drug discovery workflows across every layer:

**Target discovery → validation → drug design → trial design.** Recursion-Genentech's neuroscience target milestone is the strongest proof yet that AI platforms can discover novel biology, not just optimize known targets. The 15-month timeline from screen to validation package is striking. Meanwhile, Insilico is averaging one new DC per month and claims a non-opioid pain molecule that outperforms morphine — if the clinical data holds, that's the kind of breakthrough the field has been waiting for.

**The infrastructure layer is consolidating.** Schrödinger's Bunsen (physics-based), Anthropic's Claude Science (workflow/integration), and the BioNeMo toolkit (NVIDIA's ecosystem play) represent three distinct approaches to giving scientists AI-native tools. BMS deploying Bunsen at scale is a meaningful signal — big pharma is betting on physics-based computation augmented by AI, not just AI alone. Novo Nordisk's AWS partnership and Amazon Bio Discovery add a cloud-native layer.

**Open-source as competitive strategy.** Aureka's OpenDDE outperforms AlphaFold 3 on antibody-antigen prediction and is Apache 2.0 licensed — the moat is the Lab-in-the-Loop data flywheel, not the model weights. This pattern (release the model, keep the data) is becoming the dominant strategy in AI drug discovery.

**The Hassabis signal.** Demis Hassabis stepping down as DeepMind CEO to focus on Isomorphic is the clearest indicator yet that Alphabet views drug discovery as the primary commercial application of its AI breakthroughs. The $2.1B Series B in May now looks like a down payment on a much larger commitment.

**De novo protein design crosses a threshold.** The NISE paper in Nature demonstrates zero-shot design of drug-binding proteins with picomolar affinities. This is no longer a proof-of-concept — it's a working recipe for designing proteins that bind small molecules, with success rates of 83-100%. The implications for drug delivery, biosensors, and therapeutic proteins are enormous.

**Clinical proof remains the bottleneck.** The BioSpace analysis captures the tension: AI-native companies are raising massive capital and claiming 80-90% Phase 1 success rates, but Phase 2 rates are comparable to industry average. No AI-discovered drug has reached market. The Eroom's Law counter-thesis remains the honest backdrop. Recursion's REC-4881 Phase 2 data presentation in November and Generate's GB-0895 Phase III readouts are the next proof points to watch.

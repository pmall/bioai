- **2026-09-02** — Insilico Medicine releases MMAI Gym for Science: a series of small specialist language models (2.6B and 24B parameters, built on Liquid AI's architecture) trained for chemistry and biology tasks. Achieves SOTA on 70+ benchmark tasks across ADMET prediction (28 tasks), GPCR activity (44 receptors), kinase activity (67 enzymes), and retrosynthesis. Retrosynthesis specialist outperforms leading dedicated methods on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution benchmarks. Available via Microsoft Marketplace. MMAI Gym was first unveiled at NeurIPS 2025, presented at ICLR 2026 and ICML 2026, and an EMNLP 2026 paper accepted. Signals that small language models, when trained with domain-specific multi-task precision, can compete with and outperform specialized computational methods for drug discovery — a shift from "bigger models are better" to "right-sized specialist models." Insilico also reported 9 development candidates in 9 months (Jan-Aug 2026) and 8 clinical milestones. https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260902/Insilico-Medicine-releases-frontier-AI-models-for-drug-discovery.aspx

- **2026-09-02/03** — China's NMPA grants conditional marketing approval to Mprosevir, the first AI-assisted "Class 1" innovative drug approved in China. Developed by Westlake University/Westlake Pharmaceuticals. Uses DNA-encoded library (DEL) technology with AI-assisted screening of 49 billion compounds. DEL identified 100+ candidates, AI narrowed to 9 in days, 6 showed strong activity. Lead molecule WLU6937 optimized over 2 years. First small-molecule innovative drug approved globally based on DEL technology, 34 years after the concept was first proposed. Total development time: 3.5 years from candidate discovery to clinical trial completion. Signals China is now approving AI-assisted drugs at regulatory speed, and that DEL+AI combination screens are reaching market. https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202609/1369691.shtml

- **2026-09-01** — AdaptiveFlow published in Nature Biotechnology. AI-informed platform for ultra-large virtual screening of billions of molecules with 1,000-fold reduction in computational costs. Linear scaling up to 5.6M virtual CPUs. Screened 69 billion molecules — largest ready-to-dock library. Open-source, available at adaptive-flow.ai and GitHub. Identified inhibitors for PARP1 and FSP1 (emerging cancer target). Developed by St. Jude, University of Pavia, Dana-Farber, Harvard Medical School. Signals that ultra-large virtual screening is becoming democratized — previously prohibitively expensive screens can now be done routinely. https://www.newswise.com/articles/ai-informed-adaptiveflow-redefines-large-scale-cloud-computing-for-drug-discovery

- **2026-09-02** — MotifAE published in Nature Communications: unsupervised framework using sparse autoencoders to discover functional sequence patterns from protein language models. Introduces smoothness loss for coherent feature activation. Identifies known functional motifs, captures structural domains, and predicts stability-specific fitness landscapes. Signals that interpretability of protein language models is advancing — moving from "black box" to extracting actionable biological knowledge for protein engineering. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77333-2

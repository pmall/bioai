### Proteina signs MOU with Twist Bioscience for large-scale AI antibody validation (Aug 27)

Korean AI drug development company Proteina signed an MOU with Twist Bioscience to collaborate on large-scale validation of AI-designed antibodies. Proteina has built a closed-loop "Lab-in-the-loop" system where AI-designed candidate molecules are verified through large-scale experiments, with data fed back for further AI training. The company aims to expand monthly antigen-antibody interaction data production from 40,000 to 1 million by 2028 (25x increase). Funded by KRW 15B (2024, Ministry of Science and ICT) and KRW 47B (2025, Ministry of Health and Welfare) national R&D projects. Signals that the experimental validation bottleneck for AI-designed antibodies is being addressed as a commercial revenue stream, not just internal infrastructure.

### Ainnocence AINN-P1 protein foundation model: 28% improvement in affinity ranking from sequence alone (Aug 27)

Ainnocence released research showing its AINN-P1 protein foundation model improves antibody-antigen affinity ranking by 28% (Spearman ρ: 0.417 → 0.533) using sequence-only input — no co-crystal structure, docked model, or multiple sequence alignment required. A simple linear probe on frozen AINN-P1 embeddings outperforms a full network trained end-to-end from scratch, demonstrating the gain comes from representation quality, not model capacity. Training cost: seconds per fold vs. hours. Foundation model held frozen throughout, meaning reported accuracy is a conservative lower bound. Being integrated into SentinusAI, Ainnocence's biologics design platform. SentinusAI campaign results include 69.2% hit rate on TSLP (best KD: 1 pM), 55% on IL36R (1 pM), 51.8% on GD2×CD3 bispecific (129 pM). Signals that sequence-only protein language models are becoming competitive with structure-dependent methods for antibody engineering, which would remove a major bottleneck (structural data requirements) from the design pipeline.

### KAIST unveils K-Fold: sovereign biotech AI model, 25x faster than AlphaFold3 (Aug 28)

KAIST announced K-Fold, a next-generation biotech AI model developed by Team KAIST under the Ministry of Science and ICT's AI-specialized foundation model project. K-Fold predicts protein-drug binding structures — not just single protein structures but complex structures formed when multiple biomolecules bind together (protein-protein, protein-drug, DNA/RNA combinations). Accuracy comparable to AlphaFold3 in March milestone evaluation; exceeded existing global models on several benchmarks in August internal evaluation. Particularly strong on GPCRs, kinases, and targeted protein degradation (TPD). Key innovation: eliminates the sequence data preprocessing step (MSA search), boosting prediction speed up to 25x. Integrated into HyperLab, a multi-agent AI service platform by HITS (KAIST faculty startup). Plans to release free of charge with beta service by end of 2026. Signals South Korea joining the sovereign biotech AI race, with the explicit framing that national competitiveness depends on domestic AI biotech capability.

### PottsMPNN: protein design beyond native sequence recovery (Aug 27)

MIT Biology department (Keating lab) published PottsMPNN in PNAS — a machine-learning framework that incorporates physical principles governing protein structure and stability into protein sequence design. Key advance: enables design of structurally feasible proteins with sequences that don't resemble any native protein. Uses pairwise distributions to capture amino acid interactions and evolutionarily related sequences to teach the model how different sequences adopt the same fold. As the model depends less on native sequences, structural compatibility and energy prediction improve. Shifts the evaluation metric from "did evolution select this sequence?" to "how likely is this sequence to fold into the desired structure?" Signals that the protein design field is moving from recovering natural sequences to genuinely novel-to-nature protein design.

Source: https://phys.org/news/2026-08-ai-potentially-stable-protein-sequences.html

### AGENTEX: 34-amino-acid protein design via reworked tRNAs (Aug 26)

George Church lab at Harvard/Wyss Institute published AGENTEX in Nature — a tool for automated prototyping of genetic codes. Enables design of proteins with up to 34 amino acids (vs. 20 natural) in test tubes without genome recoding or living cells. Key discovery: tRNA aminoacylation enzymes do allow nonstandard CCA tail sequences (contrary to decades of understanding), enabling engineered ribosomes that only work with synthetic tRNAs. Includes free software, compatible with open-source Opentrons robots. Workflow: design tRNAs → batch-test in lysate → design matching ribosomes → produce novel proteins. Team envisions integrating AI optimization. Signals that the protein engineering toolbox is expanding beyond sequence/structure design into the fundamental translation machinery itself, enabling proteinchemistries impossible in nature.

Source: https://phys.org/news/2026-08-reworked-trnas-enable-protein-amino.html / https://wyss.harvard.edu/news/researchers-clear-major-obstacle-in-genetic-engineering-of-proteins/

### UCSF autism protein map: 1,800 interactions, $46M ARIA grant, three drug programs underway (Aug 27)

UCSF QBI, Rezo Therapeutics, and Gladstone Institutes published in Science the first comprehensive protein-protein interaction map of autism spectrum disorder. Mapped 100 high-confidence ASD risk genes: 1,881 total interactions, 87% previously unreported. Critical finding: despite 250+ ASD-linked genes, many mutations converge on the same small number of shared protein complexes (neurogenesis, tubulin biology, transcriptional regulation, chromatin modification). This convergence means a single small-molecule drug targeting shared protein hubs could address multiple genetic forms of autism simultaneously. Pipeline: mass spectrometry → AlphaFold prioritization → CRISPR editing → brain organoid phenotyping. FOXP1 mutation altered excitatory neuron development in human neural tissue. Three drug programs already underway. $46M ARIA grant for six-hub international network. Signals AI is enabling the molecular target identification phase for neuropsychiatric disorders that have resisted drug development due to genetic fragmentation.

Source: https://www.techtimes.com/articles/325816/20260827/autism-protein-map-charts-shared-pathways-unlocking-first-real-drug-targets.htm

### Ai2 AutoDiscovery finds immune activity in "cold" breast cancer (Aug 27)

Allen Institute for AI (Ai2) and Providence Swedish Cancer Institute announced that AutoDiscovery — an open-source experimental AI tool that analyzes massive scientific datasets to uncover new hypotheses — found that invasive lobular carcinoma (ILC), a breast cancer subtype long considered immunotherapy-proof ("immune cold"), appears to have a stronger immune signature than previously known. Validated across independent patient dataset and through lab analysis of tumor samples. First publicly disclosed hypothesis was the PIK3CA/TP53 mutual exclusivity finding. AutoDiscovery has generated 65,000 hypotheses across oncology, neuroscience, and social science. Now being deployed into Providence Swedish Cancer Institute's cloud environment for private clinical data. Signals that AI-driven hypothesis generation from public datasets can surface clinically actionable insights that challenge established therapeutic assumptions.

## Findings

### Anthropic Claude autonomous protein design: wet lab validated (Aug 18-20 2026)

Anthropic published results from a multi-target protein binder design campaign where Claude Mythos Preview and Opus 4.8 designed minibinders against 15 targets. Independent wet lab validation by Adaptyv Bio and Twist Bioscience confirmed 354 binders from 1,320 designs across 14 of 15 targets.

Key results:
- **Hit rates**: 22.6-35.1% depending on setup (vs. 10-15% typical in field)
- **RBX1**: Claude achieved 40% hit rate vs. 3.7% among 245 human competition entrants. Top-ranked design outperformed the competition winner.
- **TNFα**: Opus 4.8 designed cross-species binders (human, cynomolgus monkey, mouse) against a target that multiple expert groups have struggled with. Mythos Preview failed on this target.
- **MBP**: Zero confirmed binders from 90 designs — a notoriously difficult target.
- **Budget**: $50K multi-target campaign, $10K single-target.
- **Autonomy**: Claude ran open-source tools (RFdiffusion3, PXDesign, BoltzGen, ProteinMPNN, ESMFold2) autonomously with minimal human input.
- **Analytical chemistry**: Claude Opus 5 processed raw NMR and LC-MS data in 23 and 19 minutes respectively, matching contract lab results (96.4% vs 96.33% purity).

Claude orchestrated specialist protein design models end-to-end: chose epitopes, designed candidates, ranked them, ran in silico optimization cycles. No human-expert control arm; binding not structurally resolved. Datasets released on Hugging Face.

This is the first wet-lab validation of a general-purpose AI model performing end-to-end protein design at twice industry hit rates. It signals a potential competitive landscape shift from "who has the best protein model" to "who has the best data and experimental validation."

https://www.anthropic.com/research/Claude-accelerates-protein-design

### Isomorphic Labs: timeline clarification

Reuters reported (Jan 20 2026) that Isomorphic Labs expects first clinical trials by end of 2026, not earlier as previously indicated. Hassabis confirmed at Davos. This remains an expectation, not a committed milestone. No clinical candidates, targets, or program names have been disclosed publicly. The $2.1B Series B (May 12) was a platform bet, not a clinical milestone. One source (DeFiCryptoNews, Jul 1) claimed Phase 2 advancement in June 2026, but this appears unsupported by primary sources. The honest assessment: Isomorphic leads in capital ($2.1B round, ~$2.6B cumulative), but Insilico Medicine leads in clinical evidence (rentosertib Phase III, ISM6331 Fast Track).

https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/google-backed-ai-drug-discovery-startup-isomorphic-labs-delays-clinical-trial-2026-01-20/

### Nature Reviews Drug Discovery: critical assessment

Bender et al. published a perspective (Aug 7 2026) arguing that evidence of clinically relevant impact from AI in drug discovery is "disappointingly limited." The paper calls for benchmarks measuring decision-making improvement, not model validation. This is a field-defining self-assessment moment: with ~$60B invested and zero FDA approvals, the gap between "impressive demos" and "approved medicines" remains the central tension.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41573-026-01496-2

### Eroom's Law counter-thesis remains unresolved

The ClinicalMetric analysis (Jun 2026, updated Aug) frames the core question precisely: "The relevant measure is not how many AI-designed drugs reached clinical trials but what fraction succeed versus non-AI drugs. The conventional Phase 1→approval success rate is ~10%. If AI-designed drugs succeed at 10%, the AI contribution is largely cosmetic." Phase 1 success rates of 80-90% for AI companies are encouraging but not yet comparable because most AI programs only entered trials in 2021-2024. The field's credibility now hinges on Phase III results, not benchmark scores.

https://clinicalmetric.com/insights/ai-drug-discovery-human-trials-2026

## Findings

### Stanford AI Index 2026 Medicine chapter: protein design success rates quantified (Aug 2026)

The Stanford HAI AI Index Report 2026, Chapter 6 (Medicine), provides the most comprehensive quantitative assessment to date of AI in molecular biology. Key data points:

- **Protein design success rates** from the Adaptyv Nipah Binder challenge: BoltzGen (88.89% expressed, 28.57% bound), BindCraft (88.24% expressed, 17.65% bound), RFDiffusion (100% expressed, 7.41% bound), Germinal (100% expressed, 1.87% bound). Overall: 85.87% expression, 98.13% of designs were structurally valid, but only 9.65% achieved binding.
- **Virtual cell models** growing rapidly: publications jumped from 2 in 2021 to 24 in 2025. Performance improving: Enformer (0.38 AUPRC) → Borzoi (0.40) → Evo 2 (0.53) → GPN-Star (0.75).
- **Multimodal biomedical AI** publications: 2 in 2021 → 462 in 2025. Vision-language models expanding beyond radiology into pathology, dermatology, ophthalmology, cardiology.
- **Automated/agentic biomedical discovery** highlighted as emerging paradigm.
- Medical AI imaging models can now be trained on single 40GB GPU using radiology reports + ICD codes (Merlin model).
- The report confirms the tension between scale and specialization: smaller, targeted models often match or outperform larger general-purpose systems.

Source: https://hai.stanford.edu/assets/files/ai_index_report_2026_chapter_6_medicine.pdf

### Novamab AI (UTHealth Houston) places in top 5 of AIntibody Challenge (Aug 26)

Researchers affiliated with UTHealth Houston, competing under the team name Novamab AI, placed among the top five teams in the international AIntibody Challenge — the blinded, prospective benchmark for AI antibody design published in Nature Biotechnology. This is significant because it demonstrates that academic teams can compete with well-funded biotech companies (Aureka won first place) in rigorous antibody design benchmarks, validating that the computational tools are accessible beyond proprietary platforms.

Source: https://phys.org/news/2026-08-ai-antibodies-laboratory.html (403 blocked, confirmed via search snippet)

### Nature Reviews Bioengineering: AI-driven protein design review (2026)

Nature Reviews Bioengineering published a review on AI-driven protein design, covering the transformation from experimental to computational approaches. The review synthesizes the current state of diffusion models, language models, and hybrid approaches for protein engineering across therapeutics, biotechnology, and synthetic biology.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s44222-025-00349-8

### Elix + Tohoku University joint research on targeted protein degradation (Aug 18)

Elix, Inc. (AI drug discovery, Japan) and Tohoku University entered a joint research agreement to advance AI drug discovery for targeted protein degradation (TPD). Elix will apply its structure generation AI to TPD, combining with Professor Ishikawa's expertise. Follows Elix's April 2025 partnership with PRISM BioLab for AI-driven drug discovery for challenging protein-protein interaction targets. Signals Japanese AI drug discovery ecosystem expanding beyond antibody design into induced proximity therapeutics.

Source: https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/elix-ai-drug-discovery-company-and-tohoku-university-enter-joint-research

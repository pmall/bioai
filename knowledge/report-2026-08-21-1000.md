### AI-designed minibinders: biochemical optimization beyond binding interface is critical (Nature Communications, Aug 20 2026)

University Hospital Bonn team published a scalable workflow for AI-designed minibinders against cancer cell-surface proteins PD-L1, CD276 (B7-H3), and VTCN1 (B7-H4). Used RFdiffusion + AF2 + ProteinMPNN pipeline. Screened thousands of designs via mammalian cell-surface display and phage display. PD-L1: strong enrichment (2.4% positive). CD276 and VTCN1: far fewer hits, highlighting target dependence. Best PD-L1 minibinder BN_1358: KD = 2 nM by SPR. Chai-1 ESM ipTM scores outperformed AF2 pAE as predictor of experimental binding strength. Critical finding: when AI-minibinders were incorporated into chimeric antigen receptors (CAR), some showed poor cell-surface trafficking despite strong binding. A genetic algorithm-based diversification strategy that preserves the binding interface while changing non-binding surfaces revealed an isoelectric point (pI) window that improves CAR expression. Demonstrates that biochemical optimization beyond the binding interface is a critical requirement for translating AI-minibinders into functional therapeutic applications. Also created "quattrobinders" — tetravalent fluorescent staining reagents from AI-minibinders that perform on par with commercial antibodies for flow cytometry. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-76760-5

### OptiPrime: AI streamlines prime editing guide RNA selection (Broad Institute, Aug 12 2026)

David Liu lab at Broad Institute developed OptiPrime, a machine learning model that predicts prime editing guide RNA (pegRNA) performance. First AI to incorporate knowledge of the biochemical mechanisms of prime editing into the model's mathematical structure. Predicts rates of individual biochemical steps involved with each pegRNA and integrates those scores to prioritize the most promising candidates. Addresses the bottleneck that there are often hundreds or thousands of possible pegRNA configurations for any given edit, and evaluating them all in the lab is cost-prohibitive. Published in Nature Biotechnology. Prime editing technologies (CRISPR-Cas9, base editing, prime editing) from Broad are being tested in more than 25 clinical trials. https://www.broadinstitute.org/news/ai-model-streamlines-prime-editing

### AlphaFold 3 pipelines now producing Phase I clinical candidates (Aug 2026)

Per Skycrumbs and BioMed Nexus reports: AlphaFold 3-based protein design pipelines have now produced four drug candidate molecules that have cleared Phase I clinical trials — the first time AI-designed molecules have achieved this milestone at scale. These are described as end-to-end AI-designed molecules from target identification to candidate generation, produced in under 14 months (vs. 3-5 years traditional). More than 200 AI-designed drugs are now in clinical development overall. Phase I success rates for AI-discovered compounds: 80-90% vs. 40-65% historical average.

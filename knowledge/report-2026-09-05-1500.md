## IIT Kanpur/Baker lab: AI-designed miniproteins targeting GPCRs (Nature)

Researchers at IIT Kanpur collaborated with David Baker's lab (Nobel 2024) to design de novo miniproteins that block G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs). Published in Nature (Sep 4 2026). Targeted CXCR4 and CCR5 — receptors implicated in cancer and HIV. Method uses AI to design proteins likely to bind target receptors, then tests only the best candidates in the lab, bypassing the traditional brute-force screening of millions of compounds. Cryo-EM at IIT Kanpur confirmed how the designed miniprotein attaches to the CXCR4 receptor. GPCRs represent ~35% of approved drug targets but remain notoriously difficult to drug — this approach could dramatically reduce early-stage discovery timelines and costs. The proteins are not yet medicines, but the method offers a workable pathway for designing targeted protein-based treatments.

## Ono Pharma + Aitia: Causal AI digital twins for neurological drug targets

Ono holds exclusive worldwide option rights to develop and commercialize drug candidates to identified targets. Signals that causal AI (not just predictive/correlational) is being deployed for target discovery in neurology, where disease mechanisms remain poorly understood.

## MIT: Generative AI designs novel antibiotics against drug-resistant bacteria

MIT researchers used generative AI to design novel antibiotics effective against drug-resistant Neisseria gonorrhoeae and MRSA. Designed 36M+ possible compounds, computationally screened for antimicrobial properties. Top candidates structurally distinct from existing antibiotics with novel mechanisms disrupting bacterial cell membranes. NG1 effective against drug-resistant gonorrhea in mouse model (targets LptA protein, a novel drug target). DN1 cleared MRSA skin infection in mouse model. Built on MIT's Antibiotics-AI Project (halicin, abaucin). Signals generative AI moving beyond known chemical libraries into entirely novel molecular space for antimicrobial discovery.

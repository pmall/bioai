# Buffer — six months

This is the long-term memory of the field. It holds what would change
someone's reading of the whole field, not just this week: the events that
actually mattered, and the longer theses and hypotheses that are playing out
over months. It is the editorial line — when the short-term buffer says what's
happening, this file says what it means and where it's heading.

It is bounded, but by significance rather than age. An entry stays as long as
it carries weight and goes when it doesn't — a thesis that played out, an
impact that faded, a shift that got superseded by something more important.
New impactful developments are welcome to displace older, weaker ones. Keep
it to the dozen or two things that genuinely shape the field.

Every entry carries the exact source URL it came from, and a hypothesis
records the observation it grew out of.

## Theses and hypotheses

- **The generalization gap is the real bottleneck.** AlphaFold 3 was transformative for known structural families, but drug discovery lives in unexplored biological space. IsoDDE's step change on out-of-distribution benchmarks suggests the field's next frontier is not better predictions on familiar systems, but reliable predictions on novel ones. Deep Origin's DODock now shows this concretely: co-folding models drop below 25% pose accuracy on novel targets, while DODock maintains >50%. (Observation: IsoDDE benchmark performance, Feb 2026; Deep Origin DODock, Aug 2026)

- **Pharma partnerships are the near-term proof mechanism.** Isomorphic Labs' collaborations with Novartis, Lilly, and J&J represent the first real tests of AI-designed drugs at scale. If these yield clinical candidates, it validates the platform-to-partnership model. If they don't, the technology may be ahead of its time. (Observation: Iso partnerships page)

- **The Eroom's Law counter-thesis.** AI-native companies claim 80-90% Phase 1 success vs. 40-65% industry average, but Phase 2 rates are comparable (~40%). Rentosertib Phase III is the first real test: if it fails, the thesis that AI accelerates discovery without improving clinical outcomes strengthens. If it succeeds, the counter-thesis weakens. (Observation: BCG data via Fortune, April 2025; rentosertib Phase III, Aug 2026)

- **Three platform models are crystallizing.** (1) IsoDDE: structural biology → drug design (compute-heavy, pharma partnerships). (2) Recursion: phenomics data → full pipeline (massive wet lab + compute, own pipeline + partnerships). (3) Insilico: end-to-end AI-first (target ID → molecule → trial prediction, own pipeline). Each bets on a different bottleneck. (Observation: company websites and Fortune profile)

- **Open-source as competitive strategy is dominant.** Aureka's OpenDDE outperforms AlphaFold 3 on antibody-antigen tasks (76.1% DockQ vs 47.9%) and is Apache 2.0 licensed. Boltz-2, used by all top 20 pharma, is MIT-licensed and approaches FEP accuracy at 1000x speed. ESMFold2 surpasses AF3 on complex prediction. The moat is proprietary experimental data — not model weights. This "release the model, keep the data" pattern is now the dominant competitive strategy in AI drug discovery. (Observation: Aureka Aug 2026, Boltz-2 ongoing, ESMFold2 May 2026)

- **AI agents are entering the clinical workflow layer.** Pathos AI's Foundry uses thousands of AI agents to design trials, match patients, and optimize dosing — not just discover molecules. Schrödinger's Bunsen makes computational chemistry accessible to non-specialists through natural language. Phylo-Chugai deploys Biomni Lab for single-cell analysis and target evaluation. The bottleneck is shifting from "find molecules" to "prove they work in the right patients." (Observation: Pathos AI, Schrödinger BMS deal, Phylo-Chugai, Aug 2026)

- **The infrastructure layer is consolidating.** Schrödinger (physics-based), Anthropic (workflow/integration), NVIDIA BioNeMo (ecosystem), and cloud providers (AWS/Novo Nordisk) are building the operating systems for AI drug discovery. BMS deploying Bunsen at scale signals big pharma is betting on physics-based computation augmented by AI, not just AI alone. (Observation: BMS-Schrödinger deal, Novo Nordisk-AWS, Claude Science, Aug 2026)

- **De novo protein design for small molecules crosses a threshold.** NISE achieves 83-100% success rates designing proteins that bind drug molecules from scratch, with picomolar affinities. This is no longer proof-of-concept — it's a working recipe with implications for drug delivery, biosensors, and therapeutic proteins. (Observation: Nature NISE paper, Jun 2026)

- **Protein engineering is entering an alignment era.** ProteinDPO demonstrates that alignment techniques from language models (DPO) can be applied to protein language models for generalizable stability prediction. Raygun shows proteins can be miniaturized or expanded while preserving function. These are complementary: alignment steers what a model generates, and length-agnostic representations expand the design space. (Observation: ProteinDPO, Aug 2026; Raygun, Jul 2026)

- **Experiment-guided protein structure prediction is maturing.** Two independent papers (Nature Biotechnology, Nature Methods) show how to steer AlphaFold3/AF2 to generate ensembles consistent with NMR, X-ray, and cryo-EM data. This addresses one of AlphaFold's key limitations — its bias toward static snapshots — and opens the door to dynamics-aware structural biology. (Observation: Nature Biotech Jun 2026, Nature Methods Apr 2026)

- **The field is bifurcating on validation.** Insilico's DDD Benchmark and the Nature Reviews perspective both demand that AI prove clinical decision-making value, not just model performance. The gap between "impressive demos" and "approved medicines" remains the central tension. With ~$60B invested, ~175 programs in trials, and zero FDA approvals, the field's credibility now hinges on Phase III results, not benchmark scores. (Observation: Insilico DDD BaaS, Nature Reviews Drug Discovery perspective, Aug 2026)

- **Agentic AI for scientific workflows is the new deployment paradigm.** Schrödinger's Bunsen (physics-based, deployed at BMS scale), Pathos AI's Foundry (thousands of agents for trial design), and Phylo-Chugai's Biomni Lab all represent a shift from AI as a point tool to AI as an autonomous agent that plans, executes, and interprets scientific workflows. Unlike general-purpose LLM agents, these are domain-specific: Bunsen executes validated physics-based computations, not free-form text generation. The commercial moat is in the validated scientific workflow, not the agent framework. (Observation: Schrödinger-BMS Aug 2026, Pathos AI Aug 2026, Phylo-Chugai Aug 2026)

- **Spatial biology is getting its foundation model moment.** VirTues (Nature, Aug 2026) is to spatial proteomics what AlphaFold was to structure prediction — a general-purpose model that works across heterogeneous datasets and enables zero-shot transfer. The ability to predict immunotherapy response from spatial proteomics data, outperforming clinical stratification, suggests these models will become the computational layer for pathology and precision oncology. (Observation: VirTues Nature Aug 2026, HEX Nature Medicine Jan 2026)

- **AI drug discovery capital is dangerously concentrated.** Isomorphic ($2.1B) + Chai ($400M) = 95% of H1 2026 AI drug discovery funding. The remaining ~$130M was spread across 10 companies. Medtech funding outside AI drug discovery hit a six-year low. The sector-level "boom" masks a reality where most AI drug discovery companies are capital-starved. If Isomorphic or Chai fail to deliver, the entire sector's narrative collapses. (Observation: NewMarketPitch Aug 2026, Citeline Aug 2026)

- **General-purpose AI models are entering protein design.** Anthropic's Claude (Mythos/Opus) achieved 22-35% hit rates designing protein binders against 15 targets, validated wet lab by Adaptyv Bio and Twist Bioscience. The model orchestrated specialist protein design models end-to-end with minimal human input. This is distinct from specialized protein AI (Chai, Aureka, ESM) — a general reasoning model performing domain-specific work. If general-purpose models become competitive with specialized pipelines, the competitive landscape shifts from "who has the best protein model" to "who has the best data and experimental validation." (Observation: Anthropic Aug 2026)

- **Open-source protein models are catching up to proprietary ones.** ESMFold2 (Biohub, May 2026) surpasses AlphaFold3 on complex prediction, releases 5x more structures than the AlphaFold database, and is fully MIT-licensed. Aureka's OpenDDE outperforms AF3 on antibody-antigen tasks. The open-source trajectory suggests that proprietary model advantages may be temporary, shifting the moat to proprietary data and experimental validation infrastructure. (Observation: ESMFold2, May 2026; Aureka OpenDDE, Aug 2026)

- **Protein design is expanding beyond static structure.** VibeGen (MIT, Mar 2026) demonstrates that proteins can be designed by their dynamics — vibration, flexing, shape-shifting — not just their folded shape. This "functional degeneracy" (many sequences achieving the same motion) opens a vast new design space. Combined with ProteinDPO (alignment-based stability) and Raygun (length-agnostic encoding), the field is moving from structure prediction to programmable molecular machines. (Observation: VibeGen, Mar 2026; ProteinDPO, Aug 2026; Raygun, Jul 2026)

- **Pharma is building infrastructure layers, not just pipelines.** Lilly TuneLab ($1B+ data, federated learning), NVIDIA BioNeMo ecosystem, and BMS-Schrödinger partnership signal that big pharma is investing in the operating system for AI drug discovery, not just individual molecules. TuneLab's federated model — where biotechs run models locally, share only model updates — may become the template for how pharma shares AI capabilities without exposing IP. (Observation: Lilly TuneLab, Sep 2025/Aug 2026; NVIDIA-Lilly co-innovation lab, Jan 2026)

- **The antibody design bottleneck is collapsing.** Germinal achieves epitope-targeted de novo antibody design with nanomolar affinities using only 43-101 experimental tests per antigen. ProteinGuide enables on-the-fly conditioning of any pretrained generative model without retraining. Aureka's OpenDDE achieves 76% DockQ on antibody-antigen tasks. Chai Discovery's Chai-2 achieves 20% zero-shot hit rates and now has 5 major pharma partnerships (Novartis, Pfizer, Lilly, argenx, BMS as of Aug 20). BMS collaboration specifically targets building "AI-powered, continuously learning discovery system." The infrastructure for programmable, rapid antibody design is maturing: computational design → low-n experimental validation → therapeutic candidates. The question is no longer "can AI design antibodies?" but "which platform produces clinical candidates?" (Observation: Germinal Jun 2026, ProteinGuide Jul 2026, Aureka Aug 2026, Chai partnerships Jul-Aug 2026)

- **AI is expanding beyond molecules to entire genomes.** Stanford/Arc Institute (Brian Hie lab) used Evo genomic language models to design complete functional bacteriophage genomes from scratch, producing phages that overcome bacterial resistance to naturally occurring ones. First demonstration of AI engineering entire biological systems, not just proteins or small molecules. Implications for synthetic biology, phage therapy, and biosafety are significant. (Observation: Science, Aug 2026; Drug Target Review Aug 10 2026)

- **Two commercial models are crystallizing for AI drug discovery.** (1) High-volume licensing: Insilico's "drug dealer" approach generates 33+ preclinical candidates and partners at preclinical stage (Lilly $2.75B, SK $2.5B, Takeda, Exelixis, Boehringer Ingelheim). Now forecasting profitability in H1 2026 — first AI drug company to do so. (2) Platform-to-pipeline: Isomorphic, Generate, NewLimit build their own pipelines. Chai Discovery represents a third variant: bespoke model licensing (Pfizer, Novartis, Lilly, argenx) with federated deployment via TuneLab. The profitability signal from Insilico suggests the high-volume licensing model may reach commercial proof before platform-to-pipeline competitors. (Observation: Insilico CEO interview BioSpace Aug 2026; Insilico H1 forecast Jul 2026; Chai partnerships)

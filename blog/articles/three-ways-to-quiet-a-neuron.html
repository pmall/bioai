Protein to Pill

Three ways to quiet a neuron

11 September 2026

In one week, epilepsy attracted three opposite bets on the same biology. Biohaven paused new enrollment in two pivotal trials of its Kv7 channel activator. Encoded raised $275M to take a gene regulator for Dravet syndrome to pivotal testing. And Navion raised a $10.8M seed to design subtype-selective sodium-channel blockers with AI. Same disease area, three ideas about where control lives: gating the channel, fixing the gene dosage, or picking the channel subtype precisely enough to drug it safely.

The hold

Biohaven's BHV-7000 / opakalim, a selective Kv7.2/7.3 potassium-channel activator, is in two pivotal focal-epilepsy trials, RISE 2 and RISE 3, with more than 600 patients already randomized. The FDA imposed a partial clinical hold over a rodent metabolite finding: no new patients enrolled until the agency reviews it, while already-dosed patients continue.

RISE 3 had fully enrolled in June and its topline is still expected in the second half of 2026; RISE 2 will likely read out late. The stock fell about 13% premarket. The company points to a human safety base of more than 1,200 dosed individuals as a mitigant.

The timing stings because the asset was just licensed. SK Biopharmaceuticals signed a $795M worldwide Kv7 license in late August, and Kv7 is now framed as a two-horse race with Xenon's azetukalner. Pre-data licensing at that scale assumed a clean run to readout; a metabolite-driven hold reprices exactly that assumption days after signing.

The gene regulator

Encoded's ETX101 is the opposite end of the risk curve: a one-time gene regulator for SCN1A-positive Dravet syndrome, a severe childhood epilepsy caused by too little sodium-channel protein. The Series F, led by GV with ARCH, Farallon, RTW and others, funds the program to pivotal testing and expands eligibility to age 18.

The early signal is striking for a neurodevelopmental disease: a single dose produced 79% median seizure reduction in one cohort and 89% in another at 52 weeks, alongside reported cognitive gains. The round also funds ETX301, a neuroma-pain program to an IND in 2027, plus internal manufacturing scale-up. Late-stage genetic-medicine capital, in other words, is returning to epilepsy at the same moment small-molecule epilepsy hits a regulatory snag.

The AI blocker

Navion's $10.8M seed, led by Angelini Lumira, bets that AI can solve the oldest problem in epilepsy chemistry: selectivity. Sodium channels come in closely related subtypes spread across brain, heart, and peripheral nerves; most blockers hit several at once, which is why efficacy so often arrives with sedation, cardiac, or balance toxicity.

Its NaViGATion platform aims at subtype-selective blockers for epilepsy and neuropathic pain, from a team out of Cavion and Jazz. It is the smallest check of the three by a factor of twenty-five — and the most AI-native thesis: precision does not come from a new modality, but from finally picking the right member of a well-known target family.

What this means

Three capital stacks map to three theories of failure. The Kv7 program assumes the chemistry and dosing are right and execution risk — safety review, enrollment, readout — is what matters now. The Dravet program assumes delivery and durability of gene regulation can beat a lifetime of seizures plus developmental loss. The Navion program assumes the failure of sodium blockers was never potency but promiscuity, and that computation can design it away.

For AI drug discovery, the notable part is how small the AI-labeled piece is this week. The two large epilepsy financings are a channel activator licensed on clinical infrastructure and a gene therapy funded on clinical data — no AI-design claim at the center. The explicitly AI bet is the seed. That is a useful corrective: in neuro-excitability right now, AI is the challenger in ion-channel precision, not the incumbent. Whether subtype selectivity generalizes from a seed-stage model to a medicine patients can take for decades is the question Navion now has to answer — while RISE 3 answers, this half, whether Kv7 activation survives pivotal scrutiny at all.

Protein to Pill

Three failures at once

12 September 2026

Novartis lost about 13% in a week after three late-stage assets stumbled at once — a Phase 3 miss, a cardiovascular miss, and a cell-therapy pause. Each failure tests a different thesis. Together they explain where biotech risk now concentrates, and where capital is hiding from it.

Three misses, three theses

Del-desiran failed Phase 3 in myotonic dystrophy type 1, missing hand-opening time in 160 patients. Del-desiran is an antibody-oligonucleotide conjugate from the $12B Avidity acquisition — so the miss lands directly on the largest RNA-delivery bet of the year.

Pelacarsen lowered Lp(a) but missed cardiovascular benefit. That puts the Lp(a) hypothesis — that lowering lipoprotein(a) prevents heart events — under pressure, and it dragged Amgen and Lilly down with it, both with Lp(a) programs in the same space.

Rap-cel, a CD19-directed CAR-T, paused Phase 2 in autoimmune disease after three deaths from hyperinflammation. The safety question around rapid-manufactured autoimmune cell therapy, already raised when Novartis and BMS halted trials in the same week, now has a late-stage price tag attached.

Why simultaneity matters

Any one of these would be a bad week. All three at once show how much of a large pharma's valuation now rests on a handful of late-stage readouts in unproven modalities: a conjugate platform just bought at scale, a lipid target without outcomes proof, and a cell therapy transplanted from oncology into autoimmune patients who are not dying of their disease.

The Lp(a) miss is the most structurally important. Lowering a biomarker is not the same as preventing a heart attack — a distinction the field keeps relearning. If Lp(a) lowering without cardiovascular benefit holds across programs, the readthrough hits every Lp(a) asset, not just Novartis.

Capital reads it as selective recovery

The same week, Frazier Life Sciences added over $1.1B to its public small- and mid-cap fund, now about $2.8B. Frazier backed Verona, Alpine, Cerevel, Carmot, and Avidity — a track record built on de-risked assets with near-term catalysts.

That raise landed alongside BrainChild's $116M, Moonwalk's $70M, Solstice's $225M, Encoded's $275M, and Luma's $410M fund — what Cooley called a selective recovery. Money is available, but concentrated in later-stage, de-risked stories. Early-stage platform risk is capital-starved while late-stage execution risk is repriced in real time, as Novartis just demonstrated.

What to watch: whether the Avidity integration survives its first Phase 3 miss, whether Amgen and Lilly's Lp(a) readouts rescue or bury the hypothesis, and whether rapid-manufactured CAR-T can clear a safety bar that is fundamentally higher in autoimmune disease than in cancer.

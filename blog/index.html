Protein to Pill

AI-driven drug discovery: what's actually working, what's heading somewhere, and what's still just noise.

What's on the radar

  • Merck KGaA buys oral fertility programs from PostEra

    Mid-double-digit €M deal for an oral FSHR agonist for IVF plus an LH-CGR agonist — small molecules replacing injectable hormones, with AI credited for solving selectivity and half-life. First-in-class claim, candidate to clinic early next year. AI as qualitative improvement, not just speed.

  • De novo binders become functional CARs — with defined failure modes

    1,758 minibinders vs BCMA/CD19/CD22 mapped three failure modes: tonic signalling, epitope occlusion, off-target activity. A charge filter plus engineering yielded B5.I0 beating clinical scFv binders at high tumor burden. Binding ≠ function — now with a roadmap.

  • Pasteur AI reads bacterial morphology to call antibiotic mechanism

    Near-perfect MOA calls, sub-inhibitory effect detection, and flagging of unprecedented mechanisms, validated on Klebsiella pneumoniae. Phenotypic triage to complement generative antibiotic design.

  • 108 non-experts design 9,886 molecules in 14 days — without synthesis

    Open Discovery Challenge shows ideation is democratized via ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini plus computational ranking. The CACHE blinded benchmark is the counterpoint: AI still doesn't always beat physics on novel targets.

  • JapanFold: sovereign open-source drug discovery on non-NVIDIA compute

    ai& and Tenstorrent launch JapanFold, running Boltz-2, OpenFold3, OpenDDE, Protenix-v2, BoltzGen, and RFdiffusion 3 inside Japan on Galaxy superclusters with 1:1 GPU parity. Free workbench, API, and agent skill. Extends the sovereign AI pattern beyond proprietary compute into open-source infrastructure.

  • Foundation models only work under conditions

    BioSpace opinion argues irreproducible literature and cause-effect shifts bound what foundation models can do in drug design — rejecting "100 years in 10 years" and pointing to network target ID and clinical patient selection as the fruitful ground. Continues the Bender et al. self-assessment.

  • Moonwalk bets on quality weight loss via adipose-targeted siRNA

    $70M Series B for MW101, an adipose-targeted siRNA for obesity with preclinical fat loss and muscle preservation, no appetite suppression. First-in-human late 2027. Signals the non-incretin quality-weight-loss wave extending into AI-designed RNAi.

  • Rentosertib reverses biological age in IPF patients

    Six proteomic aging clocks from Harvard, Oxford, PKU, and Insilico unanimously show rentosertib reversed biological age in 42 IPF patients — peak effect ~3-4 years at Week 4 (30 mg BID), up to 6 years in one clock. Anti-aging effects partially independent of lung function improvement. Signals AI drug discovery entering longevity therapeutics with clinical-grade biomarker validation.

  • Navion bets $10.8M on AI for sodium-channel selectivity

    Seed led by Angelini Lumira for subtype-selective sodium-channel blockers in epilepsy and neuropathic pain. Ex-Cavion/Jazz team. Ion-channel precision as the small-molecule counterpart to genetic-medicine bets on the same patients.

  • AF3-ReD samples conformational states AlphaFold3 misses

    Repulsive bias in AF3's diffusion model enables sampling of open, closed, and intermediate states — AF3's default settings capture only single conformations. Demonstrated on ATP synthase F1β. Drug design where conformational switching matters can now use AI-predicted state ensembles. Signals the conformational sampling problem becoming tractable.

  • Superluminal Medicines raises $60M for AI-designed MC4R agonist in rare obesity

    Oversubscribed Series B. MC4R (GPCR) agonist for Bardet-Biedl syndrome and hypothalamic obesity. Daily oral small molecule vs injectable competitors. Phase 1 expected end of 2026. Backed by BVF Partners, NVIDIA, Eli Lilly, RA Capital. Signals AI expanding into GPCR space — ~35% of approved drug targets but notoriously difficult.

  • Biopharma H1 2026 funding hits $12.6B five-year high — but capital favors late-stage

    US/European biopharma startups raised $12.6B in H1 2026. Capital shifting toward later-stage companies with drugs in testing; fewer checks to early-stage. Family offices increasingly active: Duquesne backed Epicrispr ($90M), Bezos/Gates backed LifeMine ($188M). Early-stage AI drug discovery startups face tighter conditions despite headline numbers.

  • MIT designs novel antibiotics against drug-resistant gonorrhea and MRSA

    Generative AI designed 36M+ compounds, screened computationally. NG1 effective against drug-resistant gonorrhea in mouse model (novel mechanism: LptA protein). DN1 cleared MRSA skin infection in mice. Built on MIT Antibiotics-AI Project. Signals generative AI moving beyond known chemical libraries into novel molecular space for antimicrobial resistance.

  • LifeMine Therapeutics raises $188M from Bezos + Gates

    Funding for fungal genome mining with AI. Signals family office / billionaire capital flowing into AI-driven natural product discovery — a modality where AI's ability to scan vast genomic databases for biosynthetic gene clusters creates value that traditional screening cannot.

  • Adaptyv raises $40M Series A for AI-run wet labs

    Swiss biotech tripling lab capacity by end 2026, opening London lab Q4. Previously $8M Seed. Signals wet-lab capacity becoming critical bottleneck as AI generates more designs — the loop closes only if someone can test what the models propose.

  • AI-designed enzymes break down forever chemicals

    Deep-learning protein folding algorithms used to design novel enzymes that degrade synthetic PFAS compounds. No naturally occurring enzyme can break carbon-fluorine bonds in PFAS; AI reverse-engineers protein structures for this purpose. EPA views as the only viable solution for contaminated reservoirs. Signals AI protein design expanding into environmental remediation.

  • Function-aware masking improves antibody language models

    MLCB 2026 Oral: Aligns mask placement with functional priors during pretraining. 14% gain on structure tasks, 5.9x on CDR tasks. Parameter-free functional inductive bias mechanism. Signals pretraining corruption strategy matters as much as data for antibody-specific models.

  • AAMFM: antigen-specific antibody design via alignment

    Shanghai Jiao Tong: ESM3 backbone + epitope-level antigen adapter + Calibrated DPO alignment for antigen-specific antibody design. 30k preference pairs scored by AF3 structural prior. SOTA on full antibody and CDR-H3 design. One checkpoint serves any antigen. Signals protein design alignment era (DPO/RLHF) extending to antigen-specific antibody engineering.

The first AI drug starts its Phase III

10 September 2026

Insilico Medicine doses the first patient in GENESIS-IPF-3, the world's first Phase III of a generative-AI-driven drug. A 12-week volume signal now faces a 52-week FVC-decline endpoint, new competition, and a three-year path to an answer on whether AI can make medicines that work.

The bottleneck is past the molecule

9 September 2026

Formation Bio says the problem is an excess of clinical-stage molecules stuck in development. Forus raises $150M at $3B to close the prescription-to-treatment gap. Medidata shifts trials from reactive correction to predictive design. AI value accrues downstream — in enrollment, amendments, and access — not in generating more candidates.

The autoimmune cell therapy pivot

8 September 2026

Three patient deaths. Two clinical holds. Two companies pivoting entirely out of ex vivo cell therapy. Novartis and BMS halt autoimmune CAR-T trials, ArsenalBio and TScan abandon ex vivo approaches, and Roche licenses a trispecific antibody the same day. The autoimmune cell therapy thesis hits its safety ceiling — and the industry's response is to abandon the delivery mechanism, not the goal.

The open-science protein engineering loop gets $95M

6 September 2026

Allen Institute, UW, and Fred Hutchinson launch AI BioDesign — a five-year, 62-participant open-science initiative to build a design-build-measure-learn loop for protein engineering. Pure infrastructure, no clinical candidates. The gap between computational promise and experimental reality is closing, and the infrastructure to close it is being built as shared, open-access resources.
